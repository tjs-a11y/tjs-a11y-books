# Three Crypto Risk Rules Before You Place a Trade

**An original companion guide from LiveWire Money**  
For readers exploring *Mastering Cryptocurrency Trading* and *Mastering the Crypto Market*.

> Educational material only. This guide is not financial, investment, tax or legal advice. Cryptocurrency markets are volatile, and losses—including the loss of all capital committed—are possible.

## Rule 1: Decide what you can lose before you decide what you can make

Before entering a position, write down:
- The amount of capital exposed.
- The price or condition that would show your original idea is no longer valid.
- The maximum loss you are prepared and financially able to absorb.
- How fees, spreads, slippage and liquidity could change the result.

Position size should follow the amount of risk you have chosen—not the excitement of a potential gain. If the downside cannot be estimated with reasonable care, pause rather than improvising.

## Rule 2: Treat leverage as an additional risk, not a shortcut

Leverage magnifies exposure. A relatively small adverse price move can create a disproportionately large loss or trigger liquidation. Before using any leveraged product, understand:
- The liquidation mechanism and maintenance-margin rules.
- Funding, borrowing, platform and trading fees.
- Whether collateral can be sold or liquidated automatically.
- What happens during gaps, outages, thin liquidity or extreme volatility.

If you cannot explain those mechanics in plain language, do not use the product.

## Rule 3: Write the exit plan before the entry

A trade plan should describe both what would support the thesis and what would invalidate it. Decide in advance:
- What evidence would make you exit.
- Whether and how you will use stop orders—and their execution limitations.
- How you will handle a sharp move, a failed breakout or a platform interruption.
- When you will review the decision rather than react to every price tick.

A stop order does not guarantee a specific execution price, especially in fast or illiquid markets. Review the venue's order rules and test your understanding before risking capital.

## The 60-second pre-trade check

- [ ] I can explain the reason for the trade without relying on hype.
- [ ] I have checked liquidity, fees, spreads and likely slippage.
- [ ] I know the maximum loss I am prepared to accept.
- [ ] I understand any leverage, custody and platform risks involved.
- [ ] I have written an exit condition and a plan for unexpected volatility.
- [ ] I can afford the loss and do not need this money for essential expenses.

If one answer is no, step back and research before acting.
