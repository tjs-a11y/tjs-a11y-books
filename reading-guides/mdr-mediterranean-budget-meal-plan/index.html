Budget-Friendly Mediterranean-Style Meal Planning

Mediterranean-style cooking does not require specialty products or a rigid menu. A practical low-cost approach starts with flexible ingredients that can appear in more than one meal: beans, lentils, grains, seasonal vegetables, canned tomatoes, herbs and a few proteins that fit the household budget.

Build a low-cost pantry

Useful staples can include dried or canned beans, lentils, oats, brown rice, pasta, canned tomatoes, olive oil, vinegar, garlic, onions and dried herbs. Choose store brands where quality is comparable. Frozen vegetables can be convenient when fresh produce is expensive or likely to spoil before it is used. Buy ingredients that match meals you already know how to prepare.

A flexible five-dinner rotation

  1. Bean and tomato stew: simmer beans with onion, garlic, canned tomatoes and herbs; serve with a grain or bread.
  2. Vegetable pasta: combine pasta with seasonal or frozen vegetables, tomatoes and a modest amount of cheese or beans.
  3. Grain bowls: use cooked rice or another grain, roasted vegetables and chickpeas, with a lemon or vinegar dressing.
  4. Eggs and greens: serve eggs with sautéed greens, tomatoes and toast for a quick dinner.
  5. Soup night: turn remaining vegetables and lentils into soup, using broth or water and seasoning to taste.

These are general meal ideas, not recipes from the featured book or a prescribed nutrition plan. Adapt them to preferences, allergies and available ingredients.

Shop from the plan

Before shopping, check the refrigerator, freezer and pantry. Make a list by category: produce, legumes and canned goods, grains, proteins, dairy or alternatives, and flavorings. Plan to use delicate produce earlier in the week and sturdier ingredients later. If a fresh ingredient is expensive, choose a seasonal alternative or frozen option.

Reduce waste and protect food safety

Cook only the quantity likely to be used, cool leftovers promptly, label containers with the date and use reliable food-safety storage guidance. Keep raw foods separate from ready-to-eat ingredients and do not rely on smell alone to decide whether food is safe. See the FoodSafety.gov cold-storage charts for reference times.
