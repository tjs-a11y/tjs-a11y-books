Households planning a week of simple dinners

A Flexible 7-Day Mediterranean-Style Dinner Plan

A weekly dinner plan works best when it reduces decisions without forcing every meal to be fixed. This sample rotation uses overlapping ingredients—beans, grains, vegetables, tomatoes, herbs and optional proteins—so a grocery list can stay focused and leftovers have a purpose.

The seven-dinner rotation

  1. Monday — chickpea and vegetable bowls: cooked grain, chickpeas, chopped vegetables and a lemon-herb dressing.
  2. Tuesday — tomato and lentil soup: lentils, onion, carrot, canned tomatoes and herbs; make enough for a planned lunch if desired.
  3. Wednesday — vegetable pasta: pasta with seasonal vegetables, garlic, olive oil and beans or another preferred protein.
  4. Thursday — baked fish or a plant-based alternative: pair with roasted vegetables and a grain; choose the protein that suits the household and budget.
  5. Friday — omelet or frittata-style meal: use remaining greens, tomatoes or vegetables with eggs, or prepare a bean-based skillet instead.
  6. Saturday — one-pot bean stew: combine beans, tomatoes, vegetables and herbs; serve with bread or grains.
  7. Sunday — leftovers remix: turn remaining vegetables, grains and legumes into soup, salad or bowls rather than buying ingredients for an entirely new meal.

A focused shopping list

Produce: onions, garlic, carrots, leafy greens, seasonal vegetables, lemons and fresh herbs if affordable. Pantry: beans or chickpeas, lentils, canned tomatoes, grains, pasta, olive oil and vinegar. Optional refrigerated items: eggs, yogurt or cheese. Optional proteins: fish, chicken or a preferred plant-based choice. Adjust quantities to servings and what is already on hand.

Prep once, finish meals quickly

Wash and prepare sturdy vegetables, cook a batch of grains, and make one pot of beans or lentils if time allows. Store components separately so they can be recombined. Keep dressings separate from salads and add delicate herbs shortly before serving. Do not prep more than can be safely stored and used.

Make the plan fit real life

Swap meals when schedules change, use frozen vegetables when needed, and replace ingredients that are unavailable or too expensive. Keep one low-effort backup meal in the pantry. The plan is a flexible cooking aid, not a medical diet or a guarantee of any health outcome. Follow reliable food-safety guidance for cooling, refrigeration and reheating.
