Crypto Position Sizing: A Practical Risk-First Calculation

Position size connects a trade idea to the amount of money exposed if the idea is wrong. A chart setup does not determine a sensible position by itself. The planned entry, the point where the idea is considered invalid, the account's risk limit and execution costs all matter.

Start with a loss limit, not a coin quantity

First choose a maximum planned loss in dollars that fits the trader's own risk policy. This is a planning input, not a recommended percentage or a promise that the loss can be capped exactly. Then calculate the distance between the proposed entry and the planned stop or invalidation price.

Approximate position quantity = planned dollar risk ÷ (entry price − stop price)

This simplified formula assumes a long position, a linear payoff and a full fill at the planned prices. Short positions reverse the price-distance direction. Derivatives, inverse contracts, options and products with multipliers require their own contract specifications.

Worked example — illustrative only

Assume a hypothetical long entry at $50,000, a planned exit trigger at $49,000, and a chosen planned risk amount of $100. The price distance is $1,000 per unit, so the simplified quantity is 0.10 units before fees and other execution effects. At that size, a $1,000 move against the position corresponds to approximately $100 before costs.

This does not mean the loss is guaranteed to stay at $100. A stop-market order can fill below its trigger in a fast market; a stop-limit order can fail to execute. Gaps, outages, thin liquidity, funding, fees and contract mechanics can all change the result.

Adjust the calculation for real trading conditions

  • Subtract estimated entry and exit fees from the risk budget, or model them as additional expected loss.
  • Allow for spread and slippage, especially in thin markets or during volatility.
  • Check the instrument's minimum order size, quantity increment and contract multiplier.
  • For leveraged products, understand initial margin, maintenance margin and liquidation mechanics separately from stop-loss planning.
  • Recalculate when the entry, invalidation level or account value changes; do not increase size simply to recover a previous loss.

A review worksheet

Before placing an order, write down the thesis, entry, invalidation condition, stop type, planned risk amount, calculated quantity, estimated costs and the event that would make the trade plan no longer valid. Afterward, compare the actual fill and realized result with the plan. Repeated differences may reveal unrealistic assumptions about execution or liquidity.
