Pre-Trade Checklist for Cryptocurrency Traders

A pre-trade checklist is useful because it slows down decisions made under pressure. It does not identify winning trades or remove market risk. Its job is to make assumptions visible, ensure that the order matches the plan, and create a record that can be reviewed later.

1. State the trade idea in one sentence

Write what you believe is happening and what evidence supports that view. Separate observed facts—such as a price level, published network event or order-book condition—from interpretation. If the thesis cannot be explained clearly, pause rather than filling the gap with a confident-sounding narrative.

2. Define what would prove the idea wrong

Identify the price, event or condition that invalidates the trade thesis. A stop price is an order instruction; an invalidation condition is the reasoning behind the exit. They may be related but are not identical. Consider whether the market can move through the planned exit level faster than an order can execute.

3. Check the instrument and execution path

  • Confirm the exact asset, trading pair, contract type and settlement currency.
  • Check whether the position is spot, margin, perpetual futures or another derivative.
  • Review bid-ask spread, order-book depth and the size you intend to trade.
  • Choose a market, limit, stop-market or stop-limit order based on its trade-offs.
  • Check fees, funding, borrow costs and withdrawal or network costs where applicable.

4. Calculate the downside before choosing quantity

Set the maximum planned dollar risk under your own rules. Estimate quantity from the entry-to-invalidation distance, then adjust for fees, spread, slippage and instrument multipliers. Check whether liquidation could occur before the planned stop on a leveraged position. If the risk cannot be understood, reduce complexity or do not place the order.

5. Decide how the trade will be monitored

Record what would require a review: a material change in liquidity, a venue outage, a news event, a change in funding or a broken assumption. Avoid changing the plan solely because a short-term candle is uncomfortable. At the same time, a checklist should never override new information that changes the risk.
