Crypto Leverage and Liquidation Risk Explained

Leverage magnifies losses and can trigger liquidation before a market thesis has time to play out. Understand margin, liquidation distance, funding, and maximum loss before considering leveraged exposure.

A practical framework

Leverage magnifies losses and can trigger liquidation before a market thesis has time to play out.

  1. Identify the margin model, collateral rules, maintenance margin, and liquidation process for the venue.
  2. Calculate how far price can move against the position before margin becomes insufficient, including fees and funding.
  3. Compare the liquidation level with the trade's planned invalidation point; a liquidation threshold is not a risk-management stop.
  4. Consider whether a smaller position or no leverage better fits the time horizon and available capital.
  5. Understand venue outages, gaps, funding changes, and auto-deleveraging or insurance-fund rules before using derivatives.

Key idea to carry forward

Understand margin, liquidation distance, funding, and maximum loss before considering leveraged exposure.

Use this as a starting framework, then test assumptions against the underlying information and your own circumstances. A checklist can improve consistency; it cannot remove uncertainty.

Questions to review before acting

  • What is known, and what is still an assumption?
  • Which costs, constraints, or risks are easy to overlook?
  • What evidence would change the current plan?
  • What records should be kept so the decision can be reviewed later?
