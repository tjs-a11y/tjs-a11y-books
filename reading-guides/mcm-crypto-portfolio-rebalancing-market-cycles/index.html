Rebalancing a Crypto Portfolio Through Market Cycles

Unbalanced exposure can build quietly during rallies and increase downside risk during reversals. A documented rebalancing policy can maintain target exposure without requiring perfect market timing.

A practical framework

Unbalanced exposure can build quietly during rallies and increase downside risk during reversals.

  1. Define the portfolio's purpose, time horizon, liquidity needs, and maximum tolerable drawdown.
  2. Set allocation bands and a written rule for when exposure is reviewed or rebalanced.
  3. Separate essential reserves from speculative capital and avoid relying on forced selling to meet near-term needs.
  4. Model more than one adverse scenario, including correlated declines and reduced liquidity.
  5. Document the evidence that would change the thesis and review the plan on a regular schedule.

Key idea to carry forward

A documented rebalancing policy can maintain target exposure without requiring perfect market timing.

Use this as a starting framework, then test assumptions against the underlying information and your own circumstances. A checklist can improve consistency; it cannot remove uncertainty.

Questions to review before acting

  • What is known, and what is still an assumption?
  • Which costs, constraints, or risks are easy to overlook?
  • What evidence would change the current plan?
  • What records should be kept so the decision can be reviewed later?
