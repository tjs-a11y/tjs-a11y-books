What is the Bitcoin halving?

Every so often the reward for mining a Bitcoin block is cut in half. Here is what that means, why it is built into the code, and when it has happened.

The one-sentence version

The Bitcoin halving is a rule in Bitcoin's code that cuts the reward paid to miners for each new block in half roughly every four years — specifically every 210,000 blocks — which slows the rate at which new bitcoin is created.

How it works

Miners add blocks of transactions to the Bitcoin blockchain, and each new block pays them a fixed amount of newly created bitcoin, called the block reward (or subsidy). The code reduces that subsidy by 50% every 210,000 blocks. Because a block is produced about every ten minutes on average, 210,000 blocks works out to roughly four years — but the trigger is the block height (the block number), not a calendar date. Block times vary, so any future date is only an estimate.

The exact calendar day of a halving varies slightly by source because it depends on when the trigger block is mined; the block height is the authoritative marker. The sources below differ by a day on the April 2024 event.

Why there will only ever be about 21 million

Because the reward keeps halving, the total amount of bitcoin that can ever be created is capped. Adding up all the rewards (50 + 25 + 12.5 + …) multiplied by 210,000 blocks per era gives about 21 million. Rounding inside the code means the true terminal supply is approximately 20,999,999.9769 BTC, which is usually just called "21 million." The final subsidy reduction is expected around the year 2140 (estimate), after which miners are paid only by transaction fees.

Why people pay attention to it

The halving is the mechanism behind Bitcoin's fixed, predictable issuance — the reason its supply growth slows over time rather than being decided by any central party. That is a fact about the supply schedule. It is not a prediction about price: past halvings are often discussed alongside price moves, but correlation in a handful of cycles is not a guarantee of anything, and crypto is volatile.
