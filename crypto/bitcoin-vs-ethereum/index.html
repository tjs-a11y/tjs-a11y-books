Bitcoin and Ethereum: two different blockchain networks

Bitcoin and Ethereum are often discussed together, but they were designed with different priorities. Understanding the network first—and its associated digital asset second—helps make the comparison clearer.

Bitcoin: a peer-to-peer digital currency network

The Bitcoin network was designed to let participants send and receive bitcoin over a peer-to-peer system without relying on a central payment operator. Transactions are recorded on a public blockchain, and the network uses proof-of-work to agree on the order of transactions and add blocks.

Bitcoin (capital B) usually refers to the network; bitcoin (lowercase b) refers to the native digital asset. The distinction matters when comparing technology with an asset that trades in a market.

Ethereum: a programmable blockchain platform

Ethereum is also a public blockchain network, but it was designed to support programs called smart contracts as well as transfers of value. Developers can use those contracts as building blocks for decentralized applications. Ether (ETH) is the network's native asset and is used for network activity, including transaction fees.

Ethereum uses proof-of-stake to help secure the network. This is a different consensus design from Bitcoin's proof-of-work system.

Compare the basic design

  • Primary design focus: Bitcoin centers on peer-to-peer digital value transfer; Ethereum supports programmable applications and digital assets.
  • Native asset: bitcoin (BTC) is used on the Bitcoin network; ether (ETH) is used on Ethereum.
  • Consensus: Bitcoin uses proof-of-work; Ethereum uses proof-of-stake.
  • Programmability: Ethereum has general-purpose smart contracts as a core feature. Bitcoin supports scripting, but its design is more limited.

What this comparison does not tell you

Network design alone does not establish whether an asset is cheap, expensive, safe, or likely to rise in price. Market prices, liquidity, custody, fees, software risks, regulation and personal circumstances are separate questions. A network's technical purpose should not be confused with a recommendation to buy its asset.
